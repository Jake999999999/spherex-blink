## 1. Brightness vs 2MASS and AllWISE

25 isolated, unsaturated field stars (Gaia G 9–15, no star within 2 mag brighter inside 15″, catalogue quality A/B, no contamination flags). Our value: mean of unflagged single-exposure points whose wavelength lies inside the band (top-hat over the published half-power width, an approximation). Catalogue magnitudes converted with zero points J 1594, H 1024, Ks 666.7 (2MASS), W1 309.54, W2 171.79 Jy (WISE).

**Reading it:** a ratio of 1 means our numbers match the catalogue. We apply no aperture correction, so the ratio also shows how much of a star's light our 15.4″ aperture (with local-sky subtraction) captures; a ratio near 1 means nearly all of it. The spread is how consistent the measurements are from star to star; it includes catalogue errors, the top-hat approximation to the filter curves and stellar colour terms.

## 4. Repeatability between visits

For field stars of G 10–14.5, the visit-to-visit scatter of each detector's median flux: median **2.4%** over 126 star-detector pairs. Constant stars should show only measurement noise, so this bounds how small a real brightness change the app can show.

## 5. All-sky map vs single exposures

The map spectrum (time-averaged, 12.9″ pixels) and the single-exposure points of the same star in the same 15.4″ aperture, over 33 stars: median ratio **0.86** with a channel-to-channel spread of **6%**. The two are independent SPHEREx products. The shapes agree closely; the map gives less light in the same aperture because its coarser pixels spread the light wider.

## Known limits

- No aperture correction is applied; section 1 shows the fluxes nevertheless agree with 2MASS/WISE to a few per cent for unsaturated stars.
- WISE magnitudes assume a ν⁻² spectrum; stars differ by a few per cent (colour correction not applied).
- Bright stars (most demo targets) saturate SPHEREx; they are excluded here and flagged in the app.
